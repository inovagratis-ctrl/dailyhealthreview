1. What is Quietum Plus™ & How Does It Work?

Quietum Plus™ is an over-the-counter botanical dietary supplement formulated to nourish and support the complex neuro-auditory pathway that connects ear cells to the brain’s auditory processing networks. Rather than acting as a superficial eardrop, Quietum Plus delivers systemic nutrients, antioxidants, and adaptogens designed to optimize inner-ear microcirculation, attenuate neuro-inflammation, and support neurotransmitter balance.

According to recent neurological and otolaryngology research, auditory disruptions (such as ringing, buzzing, or muffled signal transmission) frequently correlate with oxidative stress in cochlear hair cells, compromised microvascular blood flow to the inner ear, and micro-damage along the auditory nerve fiber bundle. Quietum Plus targets these mechanisms through three primary pathways:

1

Microvascular Delivery

Botanical vasodilators like Dong Quai and Damiana support blood flow to the cochlea, delivering oxygen and micronutrients directly to delicate auditory structures.

2

Neural Protection

Adaptogens and neuroprotective polyphenols (Tribulus, Maca, Ashwagandha) assist in defending auditory neurons from oxidative stress and glutamate-induced excitotoxicity.

3

Signal Harmony

Compounds like Mucuna Pruriens, L-Tyrosine, and essential B-vitamins provide crucial precursor molecules for neurotransmitter balance and clear signal transmission.

2. Botanical Ingredient Analysis: What Does Clinical Literature Show?

Our medical team audited the 18 active botanical and micronutrient components in the Quietum Plus formulation against independent peer-reviewed pharmacology databases. Below is a detailed breakdown of the key botanical groups:

Mucuna Pruriens & Maca Root (Lepidium meyenii)

Neurotransmitter Support

Mucuna Pruriens provides standardized natural L-DOPA, a direct precursor to dopamine synthesis. Research indicates dopamine pathways in the central auditory system modulate signal filtering and tinnitus-related neuronal hyperactivity (Manyam et al., PMID: 15478206). Maca root provides antioxidant glucosinolates and polyphenols that mitigate mitochondrial oxidative damage in brain tissue (Gonzales et al., PMC: PMC3856628).

Epimedium (Horny Goat Weed) & Tribulus Terrestris

Neuro-Inflammation Defense

Epimedium contains active icariin, a prenylated flavonoid demonstrated in preclinical trials to protect neuronal connections from oxidative decay and attenuate inflammatory cytokines (Chen et al.). Tribulus terrestris provides steroidal saponins that defend dopaminergic and auditory neurons against free-radical damage (Alzahrani et al., PMID: 30898986).

Dong Quai (Angelica Sinensis)

Cochlear Microcirculation

A cornerstone of traditional botanical practice, Angelica sinensis contains ferulic acid and ligustilide, compounds investigated for enhancing peripheral blood flow and promoting neuronal survival under ischemic conditions (Cheng et al., PMID: 34090910).

Muira Puama (Ptychopetalum olacoides) & Ginger (Zingiber officinale)

Antioxidant & AChE Balance

Muira Puama exhibits anticholinesterase activity and neuroprotective effects in mammalian brain regions (da Silva et al., PMID: 17023132). Standardized gingerols offer potent anti-inflammatory properties that suppress reactive oxygen species in neural tissue (Azam et al., PMC: PMC4211852).

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) & Piperine

Stress & Cortisol Modulation

Chronic auditory stress triggers high cortisol levels, worsening sensory sensitivity. Ashwagandha has been demonstrated in multiple randomized controlled trials to significantly reduce serum cortisol and support sleep architecture (Choudhary et al., PMID: 28471731). Piperine enhances the intestinal bioavailability of co-administered botanicals.

Catuaba (Trichilia catigua) & Damiana (Turnera diffusa)

Cerebral Microcirculation

Trichilia catigua contains flavalignans that protect hippocampal and central nervous system slices from ischemia-induced oxidative damage (Martins et al., PMID: 29859556). Damiana inhibits central nervous system enzymes associated with neuro-inflammatory cascades (Bernardo et al., PMID: 28606766).
