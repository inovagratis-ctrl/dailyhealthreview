Why Do We Lose Muscle Mass, Energy & Stamina After 50?

Beginning around age 40, the human body naturally experiences sarcopenia—the progressive involuntary loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength, and function. By age 70, the average individual has lost nearly one-third of their youthful muscle tissue.

This decline doesn't just mean smaller biceps or looser skin. Muscle tissue is the body's primary metabolic engine, regulating blood sugar, protecting joints, maintaining posture, and supporting immune resilience. When digestive enzyme production declines with age, the stomach struggles to break down dietary meats and heavy protein powders into bioavailable amino acids.
